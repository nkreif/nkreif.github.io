About

Background

I am an Assistant Professor of Health Economics at the CHOICE Institute in the School of Pharmacy at the University of Washington, which I joined in March 2024. My research combines machine learning with econometric and epidemiologic methods to estimate heterogeneous treatment and policy effects, with the aim of making this evidence useful for targeting, personalization and policy design.

I have applied these methods to national health insurance reforms, provider payment policies, digital health interventions, mental health and economic outcomes, and the health effects of conflict and violence, in both high-income and low- and middle-income settings. I was a member of the ISPOR task force that produced the PALISADE checklist on machine learning in health economics and outcomes research, and I teach the graduate course on machine learning in health economics and outcomes research at UW.

Before moving to Seattle I was a Senior Research Fellow at the Centre for Health Economics, University of York, and before that a Lecturer and MRC Early Career Fellow at the London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine, where I completed my PhD in Health Economics in 2013. I have held visiting positions at UC Berkeley and the Kaiser Permanente Division of Research.

Research

Research areas

Three areas, with recent examples. The Research page describes each in more detail, with the projects and collaborators behind them.

01

Causal machine learning and heterogeneous treatment effects

Methods to estimate how the effects of treatments and policies vary across individuals, and policy learning to inform who should be targeted.

Recent examples

  • Using policy learning to inform health insurance targeting: a case study of IndonesiaShah V et al., Health Economics, 2025
  • Integrating decision modeling and machine learning to inform treatment stratificationGlynn D et al., Health Economics, 2024
02

Causal inference with observational data for decision making

Doubly robust and targeted learning estimators, longitudinal treatment strategies, and the use of machine learning alongside decision models in health technology assessment.

Recent examples

  • Estimating the causal effect of realistic treatment strategies using longitudinal observational dataZhang Y et al., Medical Decision Making, 2026
  • Exploiting non-systematic covariate monitoring to broaden the scope of evidence about the causal effects of adaptive treatment strategiesKreif N et al., Biometrics, 2021
03

Evaluation of health policies and social determinants of health

Quasi-experimental studies of payment reforms, quality improvement programs, digital primary care and health insurance, and of the health and economic consequences of conflict, violence and mental ill-health.

Recent examples

  • Digitalisation of access to primary care: is there an equity-efficiency trade-off?Rodrigues D et al., Health Economics, 2025
  • Psychological distress in adolescence and later economic and health outcomes in the US populationCounts NZ et al., PLOS Medicine, 2025
